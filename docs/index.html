Joakim Skovgaard (1856–1933) is best known for his monumental frescoes decorating Viborg Cathedral, executed in the demanding buon fresco technique. This drawing shows him working at a smaller, more private scale.

"Græsk landskab med huse og bjerge" (Greek Landscape with Houses and Mountains) is a pencil drawing made in 1896, depicting a scene of buildings set against mountains. Held in the collection of the SMK — National Gallery of Denmark.

The Birth of Pleasure

At the creation of the Earth Pleasure, that divinest birth, From the soil of Heaven did rise, Wrapped in sweet wild melodies-- Like an exhalation wreathing To the sound of air low-breathing Through Aeolian pines, which make A shade and shelter to the lake Whence it rises soft and slow; Her life-breathing did flow In the harmony divine Of an ever-lengthening line Which enwrapped her perfect form With a beauty clear and warm.

Percy Bysshe Shelley · Public domain

✦ Read about this

Percy Bysshe Shelley (1792–1822) was one of the major English Romantic poets, radical in both his verse and his political and social views. He achieved little fame during his lifetime, but his reputation grew steadily after his death, and he influenced later poets including Robert Browning, Algernon Charles Swinburne, Thomas Hardy, and W. B. Yeats. The critic Harold Bloom called him "a superb craftsman, a lyric poet without rival."

"The Birth of Pleasure" is a brief lyric in rhymed couplets of short, song-like lines. It imagines Pleasure as a personified figure born at the creation of the Earth, rising from "the soil of Heaven" amid wild melodies. Shelley draws on the image of an Aeolian instrument—wind through pines over a lake—to figure a harmony that "enwraps" Pleasure's perfect form, linking music, nature, and the emergence of beauty.
