Elefanter og en ung mand, der tænder kandelabre

Andrea Andreani · 1599 · Clairobscur-træsnit trykt med tre stokke
SMK — National Gallery of Denmark · CC0

✦ Read about this

Andrea Andreani (1540–1623) was among the first printmakers in Italy to work in chiaroscuro, a technique requiring multiple blocks inked in different tones. This print, "Elefanter og en ung mand, der tænder kandelabre" (Elephants and a young man lighting candelabra), dates from 1599 and was made using three separate woodblocks.

The chiaroscuro method builds an image through layered printings rather than line alone, producing gradations of light and shadow closer to a wash drawing than to a conventional woodcut. Each block carries a portion of the design, and the sheet passes through the press repeatedly to combine them.

The work is held by the SMK — National Gallery of Denmark.

Not at Home to Callers

Not at Home to Callers Says the Naked Tree -- Bonnet due in April -- Wishing you Good Day --

Emily Dickinson · Public domain

✦ Read about this

Emily Dickinson wrote almost entirely for herself: largely unpublished and unknown in her lifetime, she is now regarded as a canonical American poet. The Poetry Foundation credits her with creating "a distinctively elliptical language for expressing what was possible but not yet realized" — a description this brief lyric bears out.

"Not at Home to Callers" is a four-line poem built on a single conceit: a leafless winter tree imagined as a person declining visitors. The tree announces it is "Not at Home to Callers," promises a "Bonnet due in April" — its spring foliage — and dismisses the caller with a polite "Good Day." Dickinson borrows the manners and vocabulary of social visiting to describe the seasonal cycle.

The poem's features are typical of her work: its compression, its dashes, and its personification of the natural world in domestic, human terms.
