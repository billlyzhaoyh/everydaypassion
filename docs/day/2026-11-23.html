Ponte del Pistor, Venice

Otto H. Bacher · 1880 · etching
Cleveland Museum of Art · CC0

✦ Read about this

Otto H. Bacher (1856–1909) was an American artist known chiefly for his etchings and illustrations, though he also painted in oils across several genres. He made this etching, *Ponte del Pistor, Venice*, in 1880, during a period working in the city.

The print depicts a Venetian bridge, one of the architectural subjects that drew etchers to Venice in these years. The work entered the Cleveland Museum of Art in 1921 as a gift from Mrs. Arthur F. Weaver, given in memory of Charles H. Weaver.

Today's poem

Circumstance

FROM THE GREEK. A man who was about to hang himself, Finding a purse, then threw away his rope; The owner, coming to reclaim his pelf, The halter found; and used it. So is Hope Changed for Despair--one laid upon the shelf, We take the other. Under Heaven's high cope Fortune is God--all you endure and do Depends on circumstance as much as you.

Percy Bysshe Shelley · Public domain

✦ Read about this

Percy Bysshe Shelley (1792–1822) was an English writer counted among the major English Romantic poets. A radical in his poetry as well as his political and social views, he found little fame in his lifetime, but his reputation grew steadily after his death, and he influenced later poets including Robert Browning, Algernon Charles Swinburne, Thomas Hardy, and W. B. Yeats. The critic Harold Bloom called him "a superb craftsman, a lyric poet without rival, and surely one of the most advanced sceptical intellects ever to write a poem."

"Circumstance" is subtitled "From the Greek," marking it as a version of a Greek source rather than an original composition. In a single eight-line stanza, it tells a grim anecdote: a man abandons his noose on finding a purse, and the purse's robbed owner hangs himself with the discarded rope.

From this reversal the poem draws its sceptical moral—that hope and despair are interchangeable, and that "Fortune is God," with circumstance governing human fate as much as the human will.
