Badende nymfer i et landskab

Ubekendt · Olie på træ
SMK — National Gallery of Denmark · CC0

✦ Read about this

This panel painting, *Badende nymfer i et landskab* (Bathing Nymphs in a Landscape), is of unknown authorship. Its attribution has not been established, and it is catalogued at the SMK — National Gallery of Denmark simply as the work of an anonymous hand.

The painting is executed in oil on wood. Panel supports of this kind were standard before canvas became widespread, and they continued in use for smaller cabinet pictures well into later periods, which makes the medium itself difficult to date precisely without further evidence.

The subject—nymphs bathing in a landscape—belongs to a long tradition of mythological and pastoral imagery, in which figures from classical myth provided a pretext for depicting the nude within a natural setting.

Pain has an element of blank

Pain has an element of blank; It cannot recollect When it began, or if there were A day when it was not. It has no future but itself, Its infinite realms contain Its past, enlightened to perceive New periods of pain.

Emily Dickinson · Public domain

✦ Read about this

Emily Dickinson published almost nothing during her lifetime and was largely unknown as a poet; the work for which she is now considered canonical appeared only after her death. The Poetry Foundation credits her with creating "a distinctively elliptical language for expressing what was possible but not yet realized."

This short poem is characteristic of that compression. In two quatrains, it treats pain not as an event but as a state without boundaries: it cannot "recollect" its beginning or imagine a time before it, and it has "no future but itself." The paradox of the second stanza—that pain's "infinite realms" contain its own past and perceive "new periods of pain"—turns suffering into a closed, self-perpetuating condition.

The elliptical phrasing, the abstraction of "pain" into an almost independent presence, and the tight, hymn-like stanzas are typical of Dickinson's method of rendering inner experience through spare, exact language.
